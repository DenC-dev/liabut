Liabut: a lexical-phylogenetic study of how Timor’s Austronesian languages branch, with a pre-registered core\par}

\vspace{5mm}
{\headingregular\large\color{ink}Dennis Crowley}\\[1mm]
{\headingregular\itshape\normalsize\color{ink2}Phaistos Labs}

\vspace{8mm}
\colorbox{paperlight}{\parbox{\dimexpr\textwidth-2\fboxsep\relax}{%
\vspace{2.5mm}
\hspace*{3mm}\parbox{\dimexpr\textwidth-2\fboxsep-6mm\relax}{\linespread{1.08}\selectfont\small
{\heading\color{ink}Abstract}\par\vspace{1.5mm}
Hull groups Lakalei-Idate with Mambae, Kemak and Tokodede as Ramelaic; Grimes and Edwards (2026) place it in Timor-Babar, apart from Central Timor, and treat Welaun as a Central Timor language rather than a dialect of Tetun. This paper asks what basic vocabulary from LexiRumah says about both questions. Its pre-registered decision rule left both unresolved, and two replications with predictions stated in advance failed against Tetun; everything else is exploratory. Model-based trees place Lakalei-Idate beside Central Timor and never with Eastern Timor. Because that unrooted placement could also be Grimes and Edwards's tree rooted on the Central Timor stem, a second round, declared before it was run, estimated the root with seven outgroups beyond Flores. The root falls on the Flores or Rote-Meto branch, and Lakalei-Idate + Central Timor is a rooted clade (posterior 1.00; a relaxed clock agrees). Other results cut against that placement. Kept together, Lakalei-Idate joins Central Timor at only 70\% on the main lists; Idaté on its own leans partly towards neighbouring Habu; once identified retentions are removed, shared innovations no longer favour Central Timor over Habu; and if lenition cannot reverse, the change that defines Timor-Babar, *p lenition, needs six origins on the lexical tree against two on Grimes and Edwards's. After correction, no AU test rejects any placement, although a direct Bayesian comparison disfavours Lakalei-Idate + Eastern Timor (ln BF +11.8). By the second round's declared rule, C1 is unresolved by the vocabulary, and lexical trees here also miss about half of the undisputed sound-change subgroups. A contact-aware tree capped at five transfer edges instead puts Lakalei-Idate with Tetun and Habu and carries its Central Timor resemblance through transfer, though that model is strained. For C2, Welaun groups with Central Timor once borrowable vocabulary is set aside, consistent with Edwards (2019), although distance trees and a consensus coding put it with Tetun. All data and code are public.\par
