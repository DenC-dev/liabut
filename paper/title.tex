Liabut: a pre-registered computational test of how Timor’s Austronesian languages branch\par}

\vspace{5mm}
{\headingregular\large\color{ink}Dennis Crowley}\\[1mm]
{\headingregular\itshape\normalsize\color{ink2}Phaistos Labs}

\vspace{8mm}
\colorbox{paperlight}{\parbox{\dimexpr\textwidth-2\fboxsep\relax}{%
\vspace{2.5mm}
\hspace*{3mm}\parbox{\dimexpr\textwidth-2\fboxsep-6mm\relax}{\linespread{1.08}\selectfont\small
{\heading\color{ink}Abstract}\par\vspace{1.5mm}
Two classifications of Timor's Austronesian languages disagree on where Lakalei-Idate belongs and on whether Welaun is a Central Timor language or a dialect of Tetun. Hull groups Lakalei-Idate with Mambae, Kemak and Tokodede as Ramelaic; Grimes and Edwards (2026) place it in Timor-Babar, apart from Central Timor. This paper tests both against basic vocabulary from LexiRumah, under a protocol frozen and fingerprinted before analysis, with automatic cognates checked against expert judgements (B-cubed F 0.81). The pre-registered bootstrap rule leaves both questions unresolved. Bayesian analyses of 24 lects support Central Timor with Welaun (posterior 0.86, ln BF +10.3) and place Lakalei-Idate as its sister (0.91). No analysis gives lexical support to Hull's Fabronic, to Timor-Babar or to Eastern Timor, although Timor-Babar is defined by sound changes and is not refuted by this. Post hoc tests then separate inheritance from contact. On stable vocabulary, shared replacements pair Lakalei-Idate with Central Timor against every eastern group, and a recent Mambae layer shows in Lakalei's borrowable words. A borrowing simulation nevertheless fits Grimes and Edwards's placement slightly better, provided Lakalei-Idate borrowed more than a quarter of its core vocabulary from Central Timor. Lakalei-Idate's position therefore remains unresolved: its lexicon is mixed, and the evidence fits best an old member of a central Timor dialect chain that kept ties to the east. All data and code are public.\par
