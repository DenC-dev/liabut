Where do Idaté and Lakalei belong? A pre-registered lexical-phylogenetic test of two classifications of Timor’s Austronesian languages\par}

\vspace{5mm}
{\headingregular\large\color{ink}Dennis Crowley}\\[1mm]
{\headingregular\itshape\normalsize\color{ink2}Phaistos Labs}

\vspace{8mm}
\colorbox{paperlight}{\parbox{\dimexpr\textwidth-2\fboxsep\relax}{%
\vspace{2.5mm}
\hspace*{3mm}\parbox{\dimexpr\textwidth-2\fboxsep-6mm\relax}{\linespread{1.08}\selectfont\small
{\heading\color{ink}Abstract}\par\vspace{1.5mm}
Hull places Idaté and Lakalei with Mambae, Kemak and Tokodede; Grimes and Edwards (2026) place them in Timor-Babar, apart from Central Timor, on the evidence of *p lenition. This paper tests what basic vocabulary from LexiRumah says, with a protocol frozen before analysis, two replications with predictions declared in advance, and a second round governed by a decision rule declared before it was run. It gives Idaté, Lakalei, Welaun, Habu, Midiki and Helong their first cognate-based computational placements; all had been classified before by the comparative method. What the data show: model-based lexical trees place Lakalei-Idate beside Central Timor and never with the Eastern Timor languages, and seven outgroups put the root outside that pair under every outgroup set and missing-data treatment tried. But support depends on cognate coding and taxon choice (0 to 98\%), Idaté alone leans towards neighbouring Habu, shared innovations no longer favour Central Timor over Habu once retentions are removed, and no topology test rejects either placement after correction, although a Bayes factor under a no-borrowing model favours Central Timor (+11.8). *p lenition fits Grimes and Edwards's tree better whenever restoring *p costs at least twice as much as losing it, and the sound changes never fit the lexical tree best. By the declared rule, the question is unresolved by the vocabulary. What the data suggest: a history shaped by contact or a dialect linkage as well as descent, with Lakalei resembling Mambae and Idaté resembling Habu, each its neighbour. What remains unknown: whether either classification's subgroup is historically correct. That needs specialist review of the 47 decisive cognate comparisons, regular correspondences from fuller word lists, and independent replication; the materials for each are public.\par
