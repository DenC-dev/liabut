# Literature scan (11 October 2026)

This scan looked for research on Timor's languages that Sanak had not yet used. It covered:

- descriptions of each language;
- older classifications;
- Grimes and Edwards's own text and appendices;
- genetics and archaeology;
- open wordlists.

Two findings could be tested, and were (Addendum 5, E13 and E14, both post hoc). The rest is context for the report, or material for future work.

## What bears on Sanak's questions

### Older classifications that already grouped Idaté-Lakalei with Central Timor

- **Hull (1998, 2001).** Hull's *Ramelaic* group is Mambae, Tokodede, Kemak and Idalaka (Idaté, Lakalei and the related Isní and Lolein). Albuquerque (2009) summarises Hull's evidence and his claim about origins:
  - *Phonology.* In the Ramelaic languages *b became /h/ and *k changed, while other Timoric languages kept them: "O *b ... manteve-se em algumas línguas Timóricas e mudou para /h/" (p. 82).
  - *Origin.* Hull proposed that Ramelaic descends from Old Idalaka: "As línguas Ramelaicas, Mambae, Tokodede, Kemak e Idalaka, originaram-se de um provável Idalaka Antigo" (p. 84). Albuquerque (2010) repeats that Idalaka is "a provável proto-língua do grupo ramelaico".
- **Carvalho (2001)**, as cited in J. Fogaça (2015). This is a provisional two-way grouping:
  - Group A: Tetum and Galoli.
  - Group B: Kemak, Tokodede, Mambae, Idaté and Lakalei.
- **Why it matters.** Sanak's lexical result for C1 matches a grouping proposed twice before, by Hull and by Carvalho, from different evidence. Grimes and Edwards (2026) cite neither of them. A full-text search of the book's LaTeX source finds no mention of Hull, Ramelaic or Fabronic.

### Grimes and Edwards's own data hold Hull's *b evidence

- **Lakalei-Idate.** Grimes and Edwards define Lakalei-Idate partly by "*b > h in all word positions" (§4.4).
- **Central Timor.** They give the same change, in all positions, for Kemak, Mambae, and Liquiçá and Bazartete Tokodede (§5.4).
- **Elsewhere in Central Timor.** Welaun has initial *b > f and medial *b > h, which is exactly Tetun's pattern. Maubara Tokodede has *b > v.
- **Initial *k.** Grimes and Edwards record initial *k > ∅ for all Central Timor languages. They also note that Tokodede keeps initial *k.
- **How they read it.** They treat *b > h as having happened separately in Lakalei-Idate and in each Central Timor language. They don't raise a shared origin.

### Welaun

- **Hull (2003)** calls Bekais (Welaun) a distinct language, "structurally intermediate between Belunese Tetum and Dawan". That places it on the Fabronic side.
- **Edwards (2019)** puts it in Central Timor on five sound changes, chiefly *ŋ > g > k.
- **Sanak** agrees with Edwards (ln BF +10.3).
- **Two side notes in Edwards (2019).**
  - Lakalei *ma-p > b may have passed through *mp, the Central Timor stage.
  - Idaté has *j > l ~ r.

  Sanak's expert atlas bears out both observations, but neither is diagnostic:
  - *ma-p > b also occurs in Habu and Kawaimina.
  - Idaté *j has 3 r against 1 l, and Lakalei 1 r against 1 l.

### Areal sound change

- **Schapper (2020)** explains the morphology of eastern Timor's languages mainly by phonological change spreading within a Timorese Sprachbund.
- **Hull (2001b)** already wrote of a "Timoric Sprachbund".
- **Grimes and Edwards** call vowel lowering before *R "a regional feature of west and central Timor" (§4.2).
- **Why it matters.** Changes that spread areally are weak evidence for subgroups. That is the reading Sanak's expansion gave to *p > h, which Welaun shares with Timor-Babar.

### Genetics and archaeology

- **Souto et al. (2006).** Y-chromosome STRs from 342 men in 12 East Timor language groups: Tetum, Kawaimina, Galoli, Wetarese, Dawan, Mambai, Kemak, Tokodede, Bunak, Makasai, Makalero and Fataluku.
  - Leaving out the Bunak and the Wetarese, the samples mostly group by language. The most homogeneous groups match the broad ethnolinguistic classes, which were Hull's Fabronic and Ramelaic.
  - No Idaté or Lakalei speakers were sampled.
- **Tumonggor et al. (2014).** In 13 villages of Belu, West Timor, they found "no evidence of subdivision by language or social system" among the Austronesian-speaking villages.
- **Gomes et al. (2015).** mtDNA from 324 East Timorese, sampled by district rather than by language.
  - About 20% of lineages trace to the Neolithic dispersal.
  - The paper dates Austronesian arrival in eastern Indonesia and Timor to about 4,000 years ago from Neolithic finds.
  - It can't speak to subgrouping.
- **Oliveira et al. (2022)** used ancient genomes to infer several dispersals into Wallacea. Its abstract names no Timor site.
- **Kaiping and Klamer (2022)** assume Malayo-Polynesian speakers reached the Timor-Alor-Pantar area about 3,000 years ago.
- **Why it matters.** None of these settles C1. Souto et al. is the only genetic study organised by Timor's language groups, and it did not sample Idaté or Lakalei. Sampling them would be a direct test.

### Wider context

- **Donohue and Grimes (2008)** argue that Central Malayo-Polynesian is not a subgroup. Grimes and Edwards (2026) build on that.
- **Grimes and Edwards (2026)** say the book is the first systematic attempt to subgroup the Austronesian languages of eastern Indonesia and Timor-Leste, using historical phonology with support from morphosyntax and the lexicon (LINGUIST List 37.1261 announcement, March 2026). No review had appeared by the date of this scan.

## A correction to Sanak's novelty claim

ASJP (Wichmann, Holman and Brown, eds.) holds 40-item lists for:

- Habu (since v17) and Naueti, both from Hull 1998;
- Helong (since v16);
- Idaté (since v21), from Purwa et al. 1994;
- Welaun (since v21), from Edwards 2019.

These languages are therefore present in ASJP's global automated similarity data. Sanak's claim of the "first computational placement" is accordingly narrowed to the **first cognate-based phylogenetic placement**. No study was found that examines ASJP's results for Timor.

### E13: Hull's *b and *k changes as characters

`src/25_literature_checks.py e13`; outputs `derived/sound_change_matrix/hull_ramelaic_profiles.csv`, `innovation_matrix_24_hull.csv`, `results/literature/e13_hull_characters.json`.

**Profiles.**

- ***b- > h* (`b_h_initial`).**
  - Present in Idaté, Lakalei, Mambae, Kemak and Tokodede, which is Hull's Ramelaic.
  - Also present in Galoli and Ili'uun (Wetar-Atauro).
  - Absent from Welaun, Tetun, Habu, the Kawaimina languages, Dadu'a, Kisar, Luang, Tela-Masbuar, Rote-Meto and Sika.
- ***k*.**
  - Initial *k > ∅ is confirmed for Mambae, Kemak and Welaun, while Tokodede keeps *k.
  - For Lakalei-Idate there is no usable evidence. Its *k cells rest on at most four automatic tokens, and one of those is a misalignment (Idaté *nusaː* 'how').
  - Medial *k > ʔ/∅ also shows up in Tetun, Galoli, Dadu'a, Kisar and Sika.

**Trees (UFBoot %, 24 taxa).**

| Analysis | LI + CT | Timor-Babar | LI + ET | CT | NCT |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 8 E10b changes only (earlier) | 0 | 0 | 0 | 83 | 76 |
| 11 changes only | 1 | 0 | 0 | 91 | 14 |
| Vocabulary + 8 changes (earlier) | 80 | 0 | 0 | 81 | 65 |
| Vocabulary + 11 changes | **75** | 0 | 0 | 77 | 59 |
| Vocabulary + 9 changes, without *k (sensitivity, not pre-declared) | 73 | 0 | 0 | 72 | 65 |

LI = Lakalei-Idate; CT = Central Timor with Welaun; ET = Eastern Timor; NCT = Nuclear Central Timor.

**Reading.**

- **The *b evidence.** Hull's *b evidence is real in Grimes and Edwards's own data. Lakalei-Idate and Nuclear Central Timor share *b > h in every position, against Tetun's f- and Kawaimina's w-.
- **Why it isn't exclusive.** Galoli and Ili'uun share the change too, and Welaun lacks it. So it is not a Ramelaic-only innovation. It fits the regional spread of *b lenition that Grimes and Edwards describe.
- **The *k evidence.** Hull's *k point can't be tested for Lakalei-Idate with these data.
- **Net effect on C1.** Lakalei-Idate + Central Timor holds at 75% and Timor-Babar stays at 0, so the lean stands. It is not strengthened.

## Second round: sources supplied on 11 October (Addendum 6, post hoc)

### Kaiping and Klamer (2022), and E15: does C1 depend on cognate coding?

- **What the paper is.** It uses the same LexiRumah data and the same LexStat-Infomap coding (threshold 0.55) for the Papuan Timor-Alor-Pantar family. It checks robustness against a splitting threshold (0.35), a lumping threshold (0.75) and another coder (OnlinePMI).
- **Loans.** Their rule codes a concept as absent wherever a lect's only form is cognate with Indonesian or Tetun. That suits Papuan languages. For Timor's Austronesian languages it would delete inherited cognates, because Tetun is their sister, so E15 does not use it.
- **Treelikeness.** Kaiping and Klamer read high Q residuals with moderate δ as the mark of a dialect chain that broke up. Sanak's Timor figures sit at or above their comparison sets:

  | Data | Mean δ | Mean Q residual |
  |---|---|---|
  | Sanak, 37 Timor Austronesian lects | 0.240 | 0.0072 |
  | Sanak, 24 step-7 taxa (with outgroups) | 0.334 | 0.0048 |
  | Timor-Alor-Pantar (Kaiping and Klamer) | 0.264 | 0.0047 |
  | Chinese dialects (as recomputed by Kaiping and Klamer) | 0.298 | 0.0046 |
  | Indo-European (as recomputed by Kaiping and Klamer) | 0.201 | 0.0013 |

  The data and code differ, so the comparison is rough. Still, Timor's Austronesian vocabulary is at least as network-like as a family Kaiping and Klamer describe as a former dialect chain. That suits a linkage reading of Central Timor and Lakalei-Idate better than clean splits.

**E15** (`src/26_coding_robustness.py`; `results/literature/e15_coding_robustness.json`). These are 24-taxon ML trees on vocabulary only, with UFBoot %. "Fit" is the Mantel r against ABVD expert distances. That diagnostic was added after the trees and was not pre-declared.

| Cognate coding | Characters | Fit | LI + CT | Timor-Babar | LI + ET | Best-supported group containing LI |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| LexStat 0.55 (primary) | 1,353 | 0.90 | 65 | 0 | 0 | LI + CT, 65 |
| LexStat 0.35 (splitting) | 2,270 | 0.71 | 15 | 0 | 0 | LI + Nuclear CT (without Welaun), 53 |
| LexStat 0.75 (lumping) | 560 | 0.89 | 20 | 0 | 0 | LI + CT + Tetun + Habu, 84 |
| SCA 0.45 | 957 | 0.86 | **92** | 0 | 0 | LI + CT, 92 |
| LexiRumah's own automatic cognates | 1,965 | 0.70 | 0 | 0 | 0 | LI + Habu, 51 (Idaté + Habu, 65) |

LI = Lakalei-Idate; CT = Central Timor with Welaun; ET = Eastern Timor.

- **Timor-Babar and Lakalei-Idate + Eastern Timor get 0 under every coding.** That part of the result doesn't depend on the coding.
- **How much support Lakalei-Idate + Central Timor gets does depend on the coding.**
  - It is the best group under the primary coding and under SCA.
  - With the splitting threshold, Lakalei-Idate still goes with Mambae, Kemak and Tokodede.
  - With the lumping threshold, Tetun and Habu join the group.
- **One coding places Lakalei-Idate elsewhere, as Addendum 6 required us to report.** With LexiRumah's own published cognates, it goes with Habu (51%).
- **That coding fits the experts worst** (r = 0.70, against 0.90 for the primary), and it fails to recover Central Timor itself (0%).
- **Habu is the nearest list to Idaté in E14 too.** Habu is spoken 5–11 km from the two Idaté points, closer than any other lect. Neighbourly contact is the simplest explanation, but these data can't rule out inheritance.

### Alcantara (2015), and E16: new Idaté data

`src/27_alcantara.py`; outputs `derived/alcantara2015_idate/` and `results/literature/e16_alcantara_idate.json`.

- **The source.**
  - The data come from fieldwork in Laclubar: 73 speakers, a corpus of 800 words and 420 sentences.
  - Appendix 2 publishes 184 of the forms, in phonetic and phonological transcription. These are now machine-readable in `derived/alcantara2015_idate/forms.csv`.
  - The thesis cites Hull (1998) for the name Idalaka. It does not classify Idaté further.
- **A bias in the list.** Alcantara deliberately left out words identical to Tetun, such as *asu* 'dog' and *ikan* 'fish', to focus on Idaté's "exclusive" vocabulary. So the list can't be used for lexical comparison with Tetun.
- **E16 result.**
  - 67 entries map to ABVD concepts (63 concepts). Under the E9 rule (closest form with SCA NED ≤ 0.5), only 17 words align to Proto-Malayo-Polynesian, giving 41 tokens.
  - That fills 2 of the 11 characters. Both agree with Sanak's existing Idaté coding:
    - *p lenited: *panaq > *ana* 'shoot', *əpat > *at* 'four';
    - *b- > h: *batu > *hatu* 'stone', *bulan > *hula* 'moon'.
  - The other cells have 0 or 1 tokens, or a tie, so they stay uncoded. A tie is read as having no majority. Nothing changed, so no rerun was needed.
- **Independent confirmations outside the formal test.**
  - 'Cry' is [ˈsɛ.ɾɔ]. This is the form shared only with Central Timor, now attested from a third, independent source.
  - 'Fat' is [ˈbo.ʔuɾ] against Tetun *bokur*, with medial *k > ʔ (Hull's *k point).
  - 'Sit' is [ˈtu.ɾan] < *tudan, with *d > r.
  - 'White' is [ˈpu.tin] ~ [ˈbu.tin] < *ma-putiq.

## Third round: inheritance or contact? (Addendum 7, post hoc)

`src/28_innovations_contact.py` (E17, E18) and `src/29_contactrees.py` (E19). Outputs are in `results/literature/` and `derived/innovations/`.

**The basic sorting.**

- *Retentions and innovations.* For every concept with a Proto-Malayo-Polynesian form, each LexStat cognate set is classed as a **retention** (some form within SCA NED 0.5 of PMP) or an **innovation** (the PMP word was replaced).
- *Result.* 1,234 innovation sets and 191 retentions, in `derived/innovations/cognate_set_status.csv`.
- *Accuracy.* The rule is strict, so some heavily changed retentions are misclassed as innovations. For example, 'tail' *hiʔor* comes from *ikuR, yet it is counted as an innovation.

### E18: triplet test for contact

For three groups, each pair's count is the number of concepts on which exactly that pair shares an innovation. The largest count is the tree pair. D measures the excess of one minor pair over the other, with a jackknife Z.

| Triplet | All concepts | Stable concepts | Other concepts |
|---|---|---|---|
| **(Welaun, NCT, Tetun)**: known-answer check | Welaun+NCT 28, Welaun+Tetun 24, NCT+Tetun 6; Welaun–Tetun excess D 0.60, **Z 3.1** | 11 / 8 / 5; Z 0.6 | 17 / 16 / 1; **Z 6.9** |
| (LI, NCT, Tetun) | **LI+NCT 57**, LI+Tetun 32, NCT+Tetun 10; D 0.52, Z 2.4 | **LI+NCT 25** / 19 / 6; Z 1.7 | **LI+NCT 32** / 13 / 4; Z 1.5 |
| (LI, NCT, Habu) | **LI+NCT 58**, LI+Habu 55, NCT+Habu 20; D 0.47, Z 2.3 | **LI+NCT 31** / 22 / 13; Z 0.7 | **LI+Habu 33**, LI+NCT 27, NCT+Habu 7 |
| (LI, NCT, Kawaimina) | **LI+NCT 184**, 95, 30; D 0.52, Z 2.9 | **LI+NCT 98** / 62 / 19; Z 2.0 | **LI+NCT 86** / 33 / 11; Z 1.9 |
| (LI, NCT, Galoli) | **LI+NCT 39**, 28, 16; Z 1.0 | **LI+NCT 20** / 14 / 11 | **LI+NCT 19** / 14 / 5 |
| (LI, Tetun, Habu) | LI+Habu 14, LI+Tetun 9, Tetun+Habu 8; Z 0.2 | LI+Tetun 7 / 5 / 3 | LI+Habu 9 / 2 / 5 |

- **The check works.** It recovers Welaun's known Tetun contact (Z 3.1), and puts it where contact should be: borrowable concepts (Z 6.9) rather than stable ones (Z 0.6).
- **On stable concepts, Lakalei-Idate + Nuclear Central Timor is the tree pair in every triplet.** This is the clearest support for Central Timor descent so far, because it counts only shared replacements of PMP words.
- **Habu shows the contact pattern.**
  - Lakalei-Idate's sharing with Habu overtakes its sharing with Central Timor only on borrowable concepts.
  - That is the same pattern as Welaun–Tetun.
  - It fits contact between Idaté and its 5–11 km neighbour.
- **The excess sharing with Tetun and Kawaimina is not clearly contact.**
  - It is consistent (D ≈ 0.5) but below the threshold (Z 2.4–2.9), and it doesn't concentrate in borrowable concepts.
  - Some of it is expected even without contact. In the ML tree, Nuclear Central Timor's branches are about twice as long as Lakalei-Idate's (0.13–0.19 against 0.07–0.09 from their common ancestor). The faster-changing side loses more shared innovations.
- **Lakalei-Idate's own branch is short.** It is conservative relative to Central Timor. That fits Hull's idea that Idalaka preserves an older stage of Ramelaic.

### E19: contacTrees

- **Setup.**
  - BEAST 2.6.7 compiled from source, plus the contacTrees 1.1.0 jar.
  - The 24 taxa and 152 concept blocks.
  - Two configurations: without and with gamma rate variation, both allowing up to 200 contact edges.
  - An earlier run with the case study's cap of 50 hit the cap within 100,000 steps.
- **What happened.** Neither chain settled into a tree with a few contact edges.
  - Within 30,000 steps, both had about 60–100 contact edges.
  - By 250,000 steps, the run without gamma had about 110 edges, each moving 14–23% of the vocabulary. That is roughly 2,800 word transfers, against a prior expecting about 11% of words per edge.
  - The likelihood was still climbing when the runs were stopped (250,000 and 110,000 steps, at about 1.9 hours per million steps).
- **Result: uninformative, as Addendum 7 allowed.** Nothing is read about Lakalei-Idate.
- **What the failure says.** These data can't be described as a tree plus a handful of loan events. That fits the dialect-chain reading (E15, treelikeness), but it could also be model misfit.
- **Trajectories** are in `e19_trajectory.json`.

### E20: which member of Lakalei-Idate carries the Central Timor signal?

These are innovation sets present in Central Timor and absent from Tetun, Habu and Kawaimina, over concepts attested in both lists. The primary comparison uses the two `klamer02` lists.

| Concepts | In both Idaté and Lakalei | Lakalei only | Idaté only |
|---|---|---|---|
| Stable | **5** | 2 | 1 |
| Other | 1 | **6** | 1 |
| All (binomial p, Lakalei-only against Idaté-only) | 6 | 8 | 2 (p = 0.11) |

- **Stable vocabulary.** The Central Timor items are mostly shared by both members, so they were there before Idaté and Lakalei split. Examples: 'cry' *sɛro*, 'green' *moron*. Some of the "both" sets are heavily changed retentions rather than true innovations ('water', 'tail', 'far').
- **Borrowable vocabulary.** Here the items are mostly Lakalei-only, and they match Mambae, Lakalei's neighbour 10 km away: 'cloud' *sabai*, 'neck' *keu* ~ *tgeu*, 'eat' *mia* ~ *mua*, 'dry', 'fly', 'yellow'.
- **Two layers.** That points to an old layer shared by all of Lakalei-Idate, plus a recent Lakalei–Mambae contact layer. The counts are small and not significant.
- **Sensitivity.** Using Idaté-Cribas instead gives 5 / 9 / 3.
- **Habu, Tetun and Kawaimina.** Their sets in Lakalei-Idate number 0–3, too few to read.

### E21: how much borrowing would each tree need?

This uses approximate Bayesian computation: 20,000 simulations per model, with the closest 1% accepted.

| | Model H: Lakalei-Idate sister to Central Timor, plus borrowing from Eastern Timor | Model G: Lakalei-Idate within Eastern Timor, plus borrowing from Nuclear Central Timor |
|---|---|---|
| Posterior probability (equal priors) | 0.13 | **0.88** |
| Borrowing needed, stable concepts (median, 90% interval) | 16% (1–41%) | **28% (4–53%)** |
| Borrowing needed, other concepts | 13% (2–40%) | 30% (8–52%) |
| Share of accepted runs with stable borrowing > 20% | 39% | 70% |

- **The method can tell the models apart.** In a cross-validation added after the first run (not pre-declared), it picked the true model 87% of the time when it was H and 94% when it was G.
- **Reading against Addendum 8.**
  - The triplet counts fit Grimes and Edwards's placement better. But that requires Lakalei-Idate to have borrowed about 28% of its core vocabulary from Central Timor, as often as it borrowed other words. That is above the 20% bar set in advance, so G's placement is "hard to defend lexically".
  - Yet H also needs about 16% borrowing from the east.
  - For comparison, Welaun's known Tetun contact shows up only in borrowable vocabulary (E18).
- **Full likelihood points the other way.** On the full lexical matrix, the unconstrained ML tree (H) beats the constrained one (G) by 10.5 log-likelihood units.
- **Net.** The 18 summary counts can't separate "Central Timor descent with eastern contact" from "eastern descent with heavy Central Timor contact". Either way, Lakalei-Idate's vocabulary is mixed.

| Lakalei-Idate + Eastern Timor | 1 | 0.08 | 0.08 |
| Lakalei-Idate + Central Timor | 1 | 0.06 | 0.06 |
| Hull's Ramelaic | 0 | 0.07 | 0.00 |
| Eastern Timor | 0 | 0.12 | 0.00 |

- **Only small groups are cohesive.** Every higher grouping, either author's, has subgroupiness near zero.
- **Overlapping pairs cross-cut each other.** Midiki+Waima'a against Waima'a+Naueti; Kemak+Welaun (1.15) and Tetun+Welaun (0.98).
- **This is the picture of a linkage.** Counting innovations alone, Lakalei-Idate is a cohesive group of its own, tied about equally weakly to Central Timor and to the east.

### E23: relative dating

MrBayes ran a strict clock with no calibrations: 2 runs, 2,000,000 generations. It converged (split-frequency difference 0.014, PSRF ≤ 1.003, minimum ESS 1,003).

| Node (most recent common ancestor) | Height (root = 1), median (95%) | Clade posterior under the clock |
|---|---|---|
| Lakalei-Idate | 0.15 (0.11–0.20) | 1.00 |
| Kawaimina | 0.21 (0.16–0.27) | 1.00 |
| Nuclear Central Timor | 0.24 (0.18–0.31) | 0.49 |
| Wetar-Atauro with Galoli | 0.24 (0.18–0.31) | 1.00 |
| Central Timor with Welaun | 0.27 (0.20–0.37) | 0.50 |
| Lakalei-Idate + Central Timor | 0.29 (0.23–0.37) | 0.52 |
| Rote-Meto | 0.33 (0.25–0.41) | 1.00 |
| Tetun, Habu and Kawaimina | 0.42 (0.33–0.52) | 0.00 (not a clade) |

- **Lakalei-Idate's sister under the clock.** Central Timor with Welaun 45%, Nuclear Central Timor 30%, Mambae+Tokodede 5% (about 80% Central Timor in total). Habu 8%; Tetun+Habu, with or without Welaun, 8%.
- **Timing.** The Lakalei-Idate–Central Timor split is barely older than the diversification of Central Timor itself (0.29 against 0.27). That is a near-simultaneous break-up, as a dialect chain would give.
- **A rooting caveat.** The clock roots the tree on Tela-Masbuar, a long branch with much missing data, so the heights are relative to that root.

## Not yet used, and worth getting

- **Alcantara's full corpus.** It has 800 words and 420 sentences, of which only 184 forms are published. The full corpus would give enough Idaté *ŋ, *j, *k and *d words to code every character.
- **Purwa et al. (1994).** *Struktur bahasa Idate*: an Indonesian-era grammar, and the source of ASJP's Idaté list.
- **Mandaru, Haan and Liufeto (1998).** *Morfologi dan sintaksis bahasa Kemak*, from the same national language centre. Both are probably in the Indonesian ministry's repository (repositori.kemendikdasmen.go.id), but that site blocks automated access, so they have to be downloaded by hand.
- **Capell's Galoli notes.** Typed phrase lists and a draft paper, "Galoli: a language of Portuguese Timor" (PARADISEC, series TGAL).
- **Hull (1998).** Wordlists and lexical percentages for all of Timor's Austronesian languages. Only the Habu and Naueti lists are reachable, through ASJP.
- **Earlier phonological descriptions.**
  - H. Fogaça (2013): Mambae of Same, with a word list.
  - Hull (2003): Southern Mambai.
- **Grimes and Edwards's appendices** (DataverseNO, doi:10.18710/3CT9RO, CC BY-NC 4.0).
  - Appendix A: their language list with coordinates.
  - Appendix D: the 494-item questionnaire only.

  Their field data for Idaté and Lakalei are still not public.
- **Recordings.** Edwards's Welaun recordings are on PARADISEC (collection OE8).
